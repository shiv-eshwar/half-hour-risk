# Can the Bitcoin trade tape improve a half-hour volatility alert?

Earlier studies have already forecast Bitcoin volatility from order books, trade direction and trade size. The open question in this project is narrower and cheaper to test. Once a risk analyst has recent BTC and ETH minute bars, including trade counts and taker volume, does the distribution of notional across individual BTC aggregate prints improve warnings for the next volatile half-hour? [Guo, Bifet and Antulov-Fantulin, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICDM.2018.00123) · [Lensky and Hao, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02472)

## What previous studies establish

| Evidence | Finding and its bearing on this experiment |
| --- | --- |
| **Volatility measurement.** Andersen, Bollerslev, Diebold and Labys (2003), *Modeling and Forecasting Realized Volatility*. | They connect sums of squared intraday returns to quadratic variation and demonstrate volatility persistence in exchange rates. Here the outcome is **realized variance**, the sum of 30 squared one-minute BTC log returns. Taking its square root preserves the ranking and the upper-decile event definition. Transaction-price sampling can include bid–ask bounce, so the forecast refers to this specified measure. [Econometrica](https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-0262.00418) |
| **The case for a strong bar baseline.** Jones, Kaul and Lipson (1994), *Transactions, Volume, and Volatility*. | In their equity data, transaction frequency explained the positive volume–volatility relation. Average trade size added little beyond frequency. A model that omits the trade count built into Binance bars would give the tape features too easy a comparison. [Review of Financial Studies](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/7.4.631) |
| **The case for retaining size and side.** Chan and Fong (2000), *Trade size, order imbalance, and the volatility–volume relation*. | On NYSE and Nasdaq stocks, daily absolute returns were especially associated with counts in medium trade-size categories. Conditioning on signed order imbalance weakened the volume–volatility relation. These are same-period equity relationships. Whether nonlinear size weighting leads a future BTC risk event remains an empirical question. [Journal of Financial Economics](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-405X%2800%2900057-X) |
| **An hourly Bitcoin forecasting precedent.** Guo, Bifet and Antulov-Fantulin (2018), *Bitcoin Volatility Forecasting with a Glimpse into Buy and Sell Orders*. | The authors combined OKCoin hourly volatility history with minute-level bid/ask book features such as depth, spread and slope. They evaluated future months chronologically. Half-Hour Risk studies public executed-trade aggregates on Binance spot and scores a binary threshold crossing. [IEEE ICDM](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICDM.2018.00123) · [Full manuscript, §§III–V](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.04065) |
| **The closest trade-flow forecast.** Lensky and Hao (2023), *Learning to Predict Short-Term Volatility with Order Flow Image Representation*. | Their January 2021 Binance BTC/USDT **perpetual futures** study encoded trade sizes, trade directions and 20 book levels into images. The target was continuous realized volatility over the next **60 seconds**. Their persistence benchmark is relevant here. The instrument, horizon, available data and classification loss all change, so their reported errors cannot benchmark this project numerically. [Manuscript, §III-A](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.02472v2) |

## What the aggregate prints can reveal

Consider two possible tapes for the same minute, each with four different taker orders at the same price, each executing in a single fill. On the first tape the buy notionals are 4 and 4, and the sell notionals are 4 and 4. On the second, the buys are 7 and 1 while the sells stay at 4 and 4. Every ordinary kline field agrees, including OHLC, trade count, total volume and taker-buy volume. Yet the sum of squared notional shares increases from **0.250 to 0.320**. The signed sum of squared notionals divided by the unsigned sum rises from **0 to 0.220**. The tape therefore carries information lost in the bars. Predictive value requires a separate out-of-sample test.

Binance groups same-price fills from the same taker order at the same time in its `aggTrades` feed. The concentration calculation applies to those published groups. Executions can be split across times or price levels, and the data contain no trader identifier. A concentration spike shows how trading was executed on this venue. Identifying an institution, a trader’s intent or an unexecuted order-book imbalance requires other evidence. [Binance spot market-data specification](https://developers.binance.com/docs/binance-spot-api-docs/rest-api/market-data-endpoints#compressedaggregate-trades-list) · [Archive schemas](https://github.com/binance/binance-public-data#spot)

## What result would answer the question?

At each UTC half-hour boundary, score the next non-overlapping window using only data already closed. Define a high-risk window using the training period’s 90th percentile, then keep that cutoff fixed. Compare bar-only and bar-plus-print versions of each classifier on **identical timestamps**. A gain between those versions is evidence about additional information. A gain from changing both the inputs and the model family cannot isolate the value of the trade tape.

The positive rate can drift away from 10% after training. Report held-out prevalence alongside average precision and examine the number of alerts needed to achieve the reported recall. A model that warns on every window can achieve perfect recall while consuming the entire alert budget. Also compare with lagged realized variance, since volatility clustering alone can generate an accurate ranking. A flat or negative incremental result would suggest ordinary bars may suffice for this horizon on this sample. [Saito and Rehmsmeier, PLOS ONE, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0118432) · [Hu, Härdle and Kuo on Bitcoin volatility persistence](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.05228)

## Scope of the search

The review covered realized-variance measurement, transaction frequency versus trade size, signed-flow microstructure, intraday Bitcoin forecasting and rare-event evaluation. Firecrawl Research searches for these topics and related-paper expansion identified the two Bitcoin forecasting precedents. The cited manuscripts, journal records and exchange specification support the distinctions in market, clock, inputs and target above. The claimed contribution is the controlled six-month spot-market experiment.
